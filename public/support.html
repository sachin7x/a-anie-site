What to include in your message

Three things make a support email easy to act on. Missing one is fine; missing all three usually means a back-and-forth that takes longer than the fix would have.

  • What you were trying to do — one sentence on the task, not the symptom. “I was trying to transcribe a 30-minute meeting recording in Hindi,” not “A-Anie is broken.”
  • What happened — the actual behavior, the error message (verbatim if there is one), and a screenshot if the issue is visual.
  • What you expected — the shortest possible statement of the result you wanted.

For desktop-app issues, the app version, the operating system, and the install method (DMG, brew, etc.) are also useful. You can find the app version in the app's About menu.

What to expect after you write

  • Acknowledgement within 7 days. If you do not get one, write again — it may have been caught by spam filtering, or it may have been sent to an old mailbox.
  • Triage decision within 30 days. Either a fix timeline, a request for more information, or an honest “won't fix, here's why.”
  • Fixes on a best-effort calendar. Severity drives the queue. A bug that blocks install is worked on first; a feature request is worked on when the next batch of work is planned, and may be declined.
  • Public credit in the changelog for any user who reports a bug that gets fixed, unless you ask to remain anonymous.

What support is not

  • Not a 24/7 line. There is no after-hours support, no on-call rotation, no guaranteed same-day response.
  • Not a custom-development service. A feature request is welcome; an expectation of bespoke engineering for one customer's workflow is not.
  • Not a refund or billing department. A-Anie is free during early access. There is no billing system, no invoices, no subscription. If you have paid someone for “A-Anie premium” or “A-Anie enterprise,” that is a scam; please write so the developer can publish a warning.
  • Not a security-disclosure channel. For vulnerabilities, see Security & trust and the address in /.well-known/security.txt.

Self-serve first

Most questions are answered faster by the docs than by email. Check How it works for the product surface, Data Controls for privacy questions, and Terms for legal questions before you write. If the docs are wrong or missing something, that is itself a useful thing to report.

Languages

Support is read in English. Replies are in English. If you write in Hindi, the developer will reply in English unless you ask specifically for a Hindi reply. The dictation product supports many languages; the email inbox is one.
