Contact.

One developer, one inbox, three reasons to write. There is no ticket system, no chat widget, and no sales team.

Last updated: 3 September 2026

What to write about

The inbox is read by the developer. Three categories of message are useful, and one is not.

  • Feedback & bug reports. What you tried, what happened, what you expected. The best feedback is specific: the version of the app, the language you were using, the operating system, and a screenshot if the issue is visual. Use the form below or write to sachin@a-anie.example.
  • Security disclosures. Use the address in /.well-known/security.txt, not the form. The form is not encrypted in transit, and the form's server-side handler emails a copy to the developer, which means a security issue submitted via this form gets a permanent copy on a third-party email provider. See Security & trust for the disclosure workflow.
  • Unsolicited pitches (partnership, press, investment, distribution). Welcome, no reply is a reply of “not now,” the developer is one person and reads in batches.

What is not useful: requests for “enterprise pricing,” “a custom integration,” or “a demo for my team.” A-Anie is a free, solo-built, early-access product. There is no enterprise tier, no integration team, and no demo for teams. The closest thing to all three is this page and the docs.

The form

Up to 4,000 characters. For longer write-ups, send an email attachment.

What happens after you submit

The form posts to the only server-side endpoint on this site, /api/contact. The handler validates the input, emails a copy to the developer, and returns a small JSON response. There is no database, no CRM, no marketing automation. The email lands in a normal inbox; replies come from the same address.

Best-effort reply within a working week. If you do not hear back within seven days, write again — the most common cause is spam filtering, not a deliberate non-reply.

If you used A-Anie and want to be quoted

Real notes become real quotes in the marketing copy, attributed to the person who sent them. If you have a sentence or two about how the app fits into your day and you would like it used, say so in your message. The default is to keep the message private unless you say otherwise.
