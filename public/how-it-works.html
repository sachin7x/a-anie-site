How it works

From speech to polished text — in five stages.

A-Anie doesn't just transcribe. It listens, understands, edits, personalises, and writes. Here's what happens between pressing the hotkey and the text appearing in your document.

The five stages.

01

Speak

Hold the hotkey and talk. Speak the way you'd actually talk to a colleague — filler words, self-corrections, half-finished sentences, language switches, names pronounced the way you say them. The engine is listening for intent, not for perfect speech.

What you say"bhai kal ki meeting postpone kar do, client busy hai"natural Hinglish · filler · name pronunciation
02

Understand

A speech model converts audio to text. A-Anie's processing layer then reads that text and understands what you were trying to say — including the parts you corrected yourself on, the parts you restarted, and the parts you trailed off. This is not just transcription; it is interpretation.

What the engine sees"bhai kal ki meeting postpone kar do, client busy hai — oh wait, actually day after tomorrow bhi possible hai, check karo"raw · self-correction · trailing thought · code-switching
03

Clean

The cleaned version of your speech is produced by applying a sequence of edits: filler words are removed, repeated words are merged, self-corrections are resolved (the word you said, not the one you abandoned), punctuation is added based on cadence, and sentence structure is normalised. The result reads as a human wrote it — because a human did.

Raw speech"uhhh so the the meeting is — no wait, let's do it Wednesday, Tuesday is uh busy"
Cleaned output"Wednesday works. Tuesday is busy."filler removed · repetition merged · self-correction resolved · punctuation added
04

Personalise

Before the text is written, your personal dictionary and current tone setting are applied. Names you have taught A-Anie are written correctly. The tone — Formal, Casual, or Very Casual — determines word choice and sentence structure. Language detection ensures that Hinglish input produces clean English output when you choose English.

Personal dictionary entryKishangarh — Kishangarh, not "Kishan Garh" or "Kishan-garh" Dohdiya — Dohdiya, pronounced "Doh-dee-yah" A-Anie — A-Anie, pronounced "A-Nee"
Tone: Casual"yeah let's move it to Wednesday"
Tone: Formal"Please move the meeting to Wednesday."personal dictionary · tone control · language detection
05

Write

The polished, personalised text is inserted at the current cursor position in whatever application you are in. There is no clipboard step, no save-then-paste, no modal to confirm. You pressed the hotkey, you spoke, and the text appears where your cursor is. The cursor moves to the end of the inserted text so you can continue dictating or switch to typing.

What lands in your document"Please check if the day after tomorrow works — the client mentioned being available then."text injection · OS keyboard hook · cursor repositioning

Traditional dictation vs. A-Anie.

Traditional dictation

What you said, transcribed literally. No editing, no interpretation, no understanding of intent. The output needs a human editor before it is useful.

  • Transcribes exactly what was said
  • Preserves filler words and repetition
  • Keeps self-corrections verbatim
  • No tone or language customisation
  • No personal dictionary support
  • No understanding of code-switching

A-Anie

What you meant, written clearly. The engine listens for intent, resolves ambiguity, applies your personal settings, and writes output that doesn't need editing.

  • Writes what you meant, not what you said
  • Removes filler words automatically
  • Resolves self-corrections intelligently
  • Tone control: Formal, Casual, Very Casual
  • Personal dictionary with your names and terms
  • Full Hinglish and Indian English support
